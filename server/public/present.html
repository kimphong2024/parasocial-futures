The method underneath

Real-time Bayesian foresight

Bayesian thinking holds a belief as a probability, not a verdict: you state it before the evidence, in a form specific enough to be moved, then let each new observation shift it. You never arrive at certainty — only at a better-informed position, held until something moves it again.

Prior→Evidence→Posterior …which becomes tomorrow's prior

How this platform meets it

01
Priors are stated, not implied

Eight drivers, each a distribution — minimum, most-likely, maximum — with a written rationale naming the signal clusters behind the range.

02
Evidence arrives nightly

The scan runs every night and a human decides what enters the library. Beliefs meet new observations continuously, not once at the start of a project.

03
Updating is a single run

Move a range and ten thousand futures re-sample; every scenario's odds move with it, seeded so the update is exactly reproducible.

04
It names what to watch

The sensitivity chart ranks which belief, if wrong, would most redraw the map — telling you which evidence is worth going to find next.

No number here is a forecast. Each is today's position, held until the evidence moves it.

Decision support · simulation

Odds in ten thousand futures

Every driver is spun once from its own range to build one complete future — then ten thousand of them. Each is checked against every scenario's declared conditions; the share that lands inside a scenario is its probability.

10,000 sampled futuresseeded · reproducible~40 ms a runresidual reported, not hidden

Change a belief and the whole map redraws — which is the point. The sensitivity chart then ranks which belief, if wrong, would move the answer most.

Decision support · chat

Ask the futures

A question is embedded, matched against the whole reviewed library, reranked, and answered alongside the published scenarios — so the reply argues from the evidence base rather than from the model's memory.
